How the lessons build the SVS skills

Grade 4 children typically sit between Level 2 and Level 3. Each lesson names the SVS skills it practises and gives look-fors so you can notice, name and celebrate the behaviour (“I saw you ask a teammate for help. That is Level 3!”). Use the “I” statements as exit-talk with children.

How to use SVS during the lesson

• Before the group task, name the target: “Today we practise Build Community: we ask for help when we need it.”
• While circulating, tally look-fors on a sticky note.
• In the closing, praise 2–3 specific behaviours and ask: “Which level did your team reach today?”

Tips for a smooth lesson

• Set group roles BEFORE the activity starts.
• Use a 30-second countdown (“3-2-1 freeze!”) for transitions.
• For pre-emergent learners, start orally, then add pictures, then words.
• Keep the penguin Noot (a toy or drawing) as your class mascot.

NOUNS • GRADE 4 • 35 MINUTES

Name It to Claim It!

Practice: Worksheets 1 & 2 (Worksheet 16 for mixed revision)
Spark 5
KLA 10
Assessment 18 (group work + individual check)
5 Magic QuestionsSteps (35 mins)Plan
LESSON PLANPurposeObjectiveI can find nouns (naming words) and use them in my own sentences.
Big QuestionWhat would happen if nothing in the world had a name?
IntegrationWhy

Hook: “Hands up if you have ever lost your bag, your bottle or your pet. What did you tell Mum or Dad? You had to NAME it!”

Every time we ask for a snack, call a friend, tell someone where we left something, or send a message, we use nouns. Without them nobody knows WHO or WHAT we mean. Today we become Name Masters: people who can say exactly what they mean in stories, shops, games and real life.

MethodSpark(5 mins)
  • 0:00 The Thingamajig Teacher. Walk in and say, very seriously: “Good morning! Please take out your whatchamacallit from your thingy and put it on your wobbly-bob.” Use NO real nouns for 60 seconds. Students try to follow and giggle.
  • 0:01 Ask: “Why couldn’t you do it?” (We didn’t have the names!)
  • 0:02 Name Blast! Point at things in the room. In 30 seconds the class shouts the real name of each. Write the best 6 words on the board and label them NOUNS.
  • 0:04 Reveal the Big Question + objective. Class reads the objective in 3 voices: robot, whisper, superhero.
KLA(10-12 mins)
  • I Do (2 min): Four corners with doodle posters: Person 👩‍🏫, Place 🏫, Animal 🐶, Thing ⚽. Teach a body sign for each (Person = stand tall, Place = arms make a roof, Animal = claws, Thing = hold an imaginary object).
  • We Do – Noun Corners (5 min): Teacher reads or shows 10 word/picture cards (teacher, Mumbai, tiger, bag, school, Meera, rabbit, kite, doctor, river). Students penguin-walk to the right corner and freeze with the sign. Pause on tricky ones (birthday? idea?) and let kids argue it out politely.
  • You Do (3 min): At their corner, each group has 60 seconds to add 4 more nouns (write or draw) on the corner chart, then rotates once and adds 2 more.
  • One-line add-on: “Special names like Meera, India, Diwali are VIP nouns: they get a CAPITAL letter.”

VERBS • GRADE 4 • 35 MINUTES

Action! Make It Happen!

Practice: Worksheets 3 & 4
Spark 5
KLA 10
Assessment 18 (group work + individual check)
5 Magic QuestionsSteps (35 mins)Plan
LESSON PLANPurposeObjectiveI can find verbs (action words) and say if something happens now or before.
Big QuestionCan a sentence come alive without an action?
IntegrationWhy

Hook: “Imagine a video game where nobody can run, jump or throw. Or a cooking video where nobody cooks!”

Every story, game, recipe and instruction (“Throw the ball!”, “Stir the dal!”) runs on verbs. Without them, writing is a frozen photo. Today we become Action Heroes who make sentences MOVE and can also say WHEN things happen, like telling your teacher what you did last weekend.

MethodSpark(5 mins)
  • 0:00 Freeze Frame! Play music or clap a beat; students dance. Clap = FREEZE.
  • 0:02 Pick 3 frozen kids: “Describe the picture with NO action words.” (girl… ball… table…) It sounds flat and boring!
  • 0:03 “ACTION!” Shout verbs and everybody does them on the spot: spin, hop, sleep, clap, swim. “What made the room come alive?” Write 5 verbs on the board: VERBS.
  • 0:05 Reveal Big Question + objective.
KLA(10-12 mins)
  • Verb Charades (4 min): Teacher mimes (kick, brush, read, climb). Class calls the verb and says a sentence: “The boy kicks the ball.” Then 3 volunteers mime for the class.
  • Time Machine (5 min): Tape a line on the floor: YESTERDAY ◀ TODAY ▶. Teacher says “I walk” (stand on TODAY). “I walked” (step to YESTERDAY). Class jumps the line with jump/jumped, play/played. Teach: most verbs just add -ed.
  • Rebel Verbs (1 min): Some verbs are rebels and change completely: go → went, eat → ate, run → ran, see → saw. Class chants them with a sound effect.

ADJECTIVES • GRADE 4 • 35 MINUTES

Wow Words: Describe Like a Superstar!

Practice: Worksheets 5 & 6 (Worksheet 16 for mixed revision)
Spark 5
KLA 10
Assessment 18 (group work + individual check)
5 Magic QuestionsSteps (35 mins)Plan
LESSON PLANPurposeObjectiveI can use adjectives (describing words) to paint a picture with words.
Big QuestionHow can I make someone SEE a picture that only exists in my head?
IntegrationWhy

Hook: “If I say ‘I got a dog’… what do you see? Now: ‘I got a tiny, fluffy, golden puppy.’ What do you see NOW?”

Game designers, cartoonists, food bloggers and YouTubers all use adjectives so that people can imagine, choose and get excited (“a spicy, crunchy samosa!”). Adjectives also help us describe ourselves (brave, curious, kind) and understand how others feel.

MethodSpark(5 mins)
  • 0:00 Mystery Bag. Put an object in a cloth bag (a mango, a soft toy, a spiky ball, a stone). Three kids feel it and describe WITHOUT naming it: “soft… round… bumpy…” The class guesses.
  • 0:03 Boring vs WOW. Board: “I saw a dog.” vs “I saw a tiny, fluffy, golden dog.” Class shows a bored face for the first and a WOW face for the second.
  • 0:05 “What did we use to paint the picture? Adjectives!” Reveal Big Question + objective.
KLA(10-12 mins)
  • Sentence Stretch (4 min): Board: “The cat sat.” Add one adjective at a time. Students stretch their arms wider with each one: “The lazy cat sat.” → “The lazy orange cat sat on the soft mat.”
  • Five Senses Wow Wall (4 min): Five chart sheets: SEE 👀 TOUCH ✋ TASTE 👅 SMELL 👃 HEAR 👂. Teacher names a noun (ice-cream, rain, drum); groups run to stick sticky notes with adjectives on the right sheet.
  • Big, Bigger, Biggest (2 min): Three volunteers line up: tall, taller, tallest. Use -er for 2 things and -est for 3+.

Pronoun Power: Stop the Repeat-Robot!

Practice: Worksheets 7 & 8 (Worksheet 17 for mixed revision)
Spark 5
KLA 10
Assessment 18 (group work + individual check)
5 Magic QuestionsSteps (35 mins)Plan
LESSON PLANPurposeObjectiveI can use pronouns (he, she, it, they, we, I, you) and my, your, his, her, our, their so I don’t repeat names.
Big QuestionWhy don’t we say “Meera” ten times in one story?
IntegrationWhy

Hook: “Imagine telling your friend: ‘Rahul went to Rahul’s house and Rahul took Rahul’s bag.’ Would they stay to listen?”

Pronouns make our speaking and writing smooth, quick and natural: in stories, chats and messages. They also help us show respect, because we use the pronoun that each person uses for themselves. Today we beat the Repeat-Robot!

MethodSpark(5 mins)
  • 0:00 The Repeat-Robot. Teacher speaks like a robot: “Meera has a kite. Meera’s kite is red. Meera flies Meera’s kite. Meera loves Meera’s kite.”
  • 0:02 Students press an imaginary BUZZER (“BZZT!”) every time a name repeats. Count the buzzes.
  • 0:03 Fix the robot! Class re-tells the story: “Meera has a kite. It is red. She flies it. She loves her kite.” Cheer!
  • 0:05 Reveal Big Question + objective.
KLA(10-12 mins)
  • Pronoun Gestures (3 min): I = hand on chest • you = point to a friend • he/she = point to a boy/girl across the room • it = point to an object • we = group hug • they = wave at a group. Teacher says “Meera”, “Arjun and Rita”, “my pencil”, “you and me”; class does the gesture + says the pronoun.
  • Whose is it? (4 min): Basket with a bag, bottle, pencil, book. Hold up each item: “Whose is this? It is his bag. It is her bottle. It is our book. It is their pencil.” Chart: my, your, his, her, its, our, their with doodles.
  • Pronoun Swap (3 min): Teacher says a sentence with a name; class replies in chorus with the pronoun version (“Rohan eats rice.” → “He eats rice.”).

A, An, The: Tiny Words, Big Jobs!

Practice: Worksheet 9 (Worksheet 17 for mixed revision)
Spark 5
KLA 10
Assessment 18 (group work + individual check)
5 Magic QuestionsSteps (35 mins)Plan
LESSON PLANPurposeObjectiveI can choose a, an or the correctly when I speak and write.
Big QuestionWhy do we say “an apple” but “a banana”?
IntegrationWhy

Hook: “Say it fast: ‘I want a apple.’ Did your tongue trip?”

An makes words glide smoothly. And a vs the tells your listener if you mean any one or one special one: “Give me a pen” (any pen) vs “Give me the pen” (THAT pen). At a shop, while ordering lunch, in a game or when giving directions, these tiny words stop big confusion.

MethodSpark(5 mins)
  • 0:00 The Hiccup Game. Teacher says: “I ate a apple, a egg and a ice-cream.” Students HICCUP loudly at every mistake and shout the fix (“AN!”).
  • 0:02 Any vs THE. “Bring me a pencil.” (any pencil arrives!) “Bring me the pencil.” (they freeze: which one?) “The pencil on Rohan’s desk.” Now everyone knows exactly which. Ha!
  • 0:05 Reveal Big Question + objective.
KLA(10-12 mins)
  • Vowel Doors (6 min): Tape two doors on the floor: A-DOOR (consonant sounds) and AN-DOOR (vowel sounds a, e, i, o, u). Show word cards: apple, banana, umbrella, zebra, elephant, kite, orange, egg, owl, pencil, ice-cream, ant. Students hop through the correct door and say “an apple!”. Rule: listen for the SOUND, not just the letter.
  • THE = Spotlight (3 min): Use a torch or finger-spotlight: “The sun, the moon, the board.” Use the for one special thing, or something already mentioned: “I saw a dog. The dog was big.”
  • Doodle rule chart (1 min): a / an / the, drawn together on the board.

PREPOSITIONS • GRADE 4 • 35 MINUTES

Where in the World? The Treasure Hunt

Practice: Worksheets 10 & 11 (Worksheet 17 for mixed revision)
Spark 5
KLA 10
Assessment 18 (group work + individual check)
5 Magic QuestionsSteps (35 mins)Plan
LESSON PLANPurposeObjectiveI can use prepositions (in, on, under, behind, between…) to say exactly where something is.
Big QuestionHow can words lead someone to hidden treasure?
IntegrationWhy

Hook: “Where is my shoe?” “It’s… the bed.” (Under? On? Behind?) Without the little word, the clue is useless!

A treasure map, hide-and-seek, giving a friend directions to your house, telling Mum where you left your lunchbox: all need prepositions. They are the GPS words of English.

MethodSpark(5 mins)
  • 0:00 Simon Says: Preposition Edition. “Simon says put your pencil ON your head… UNDER your chair… BEHIND your back… BETWEEN two fingers… IN FRONT OF your nose… NEXT TO your friend.” Nobody is out; a wrong move earns a funny penguin wobble.
  • 0:04 Ask: “Which words told you WHERE?” Write them on the board.

Big QuestionHow can tiny glue words join ideas… and even people?
IntegrationWhy

Hook: “I am hungry. I eat. It is hot. I wait.” Sounds like a choppy robot! Now: “I was hungry so I ate, but it was hot so I waited.”

Conjunctions let us give reasons, make choices, explain ourselves and even disagree politely (“I understand, but…”). Our Constitution speaks of Fraternity: people joined like friends. Conjunctions join words; friendship joins people.

MethodSpark(5 mins)
  • 0:00 Choppy Robot vs Smooth Speaker. Teacher speaks in short robotic sentences; class says “bzzt!” Then the smooth version; class cheers.
  • 0:02 Human Glue. Two students hold sentence cards (“It was raining.” / “We played outside.”). A third student with a but card steps in between as GLUE. Class reads the joined sentence. Swap in so: “It was raining so we stayed inside.” Meaning changes!
  • 0:05 Reveal Big Question + objective.
KLA(10-12 mins)
  • Glue Gestures (3 min): and = clap hands together ➕ • but = push palms apart ↔ • or = hands like weighing scales 🔀 • because = point to head (reason) 💡 • so = arrow forward 🎯.
  • Which glue? (5 min): Teacher reads half-sentences (“I was hungry ___ I ate.” “We stayed inside ___ it was raining.”). Class shows the gesture; teacher reveals the glue word card.
  • Finish My Sentence Relay (2 min): Teams line up. Teacher gives starters (“I wanted to play but…”, “I was happy because…”). First child completes it in 5 seconds and goes to the back of the line.
