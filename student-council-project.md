# Student Council Project

> **Status:** First draft. Items marked *(to confirm)* need details from the school context.

## 1. Overview

A training curriculum for my school's student council. The aim is to give each student leader a clear understanding of their role, their responsibilities, and exactly how to carry them out. I will teach the curriculum, guide the council as they put it into practice, and then assess its impact on their confidence and on how they feel as council members.

## 2. The problem

- Council members do not have a clear understanding of their roles, responsibilities, or how to perform them.
- There is a lot of indiscipline among students and a lack of structure in the school.
- Fights are constant.
- Students do not respect or listen to the student council.

## 3. Purpose

1. Shift the status of the student council, so it is seen as a body with real meaning and authority.
2. Add gravitas to every student leader's responsibilities by stating clearly **what** should be done and **how**.
3. Build confident student leaders who can perform their duties and guide their peers.

## 4. Who it is for

Student council members at my school. *(to confirm: grade levels, number of members, and the positions that exist)*

## 5. Curriculum framework (proposed)

Each module ends with a short practice task so members apply the idea in school, not only learn it.

| # | Module | What members learn | Practice task |
|---|--------|--------------------|---------------|
| 1 | What it means to lead | Why the council exists; leader versus boss; the responsibility that comes with the badge | Write a personal leadership promise |
| 2 | My role, my responsibilities | A role card for each position: what to do, when, and how | Fill in and present their own role card |
| 3 | Earning respect | Leading by example; fairness; consistency; how peers decide whether to listen | Self-audit of their own daily habits |
| 4 | Communication and presence | Clear instructions, a calm voice, body language, speaking to a group | Give a one-minute announcement |
| 5 | Keeping order | Maintaining routines and structure in assemblies, queues, corridors, and break time; what to do and what to hand to a teacher | Role-play common situations |
| 6 | Handling conflict | De-escalating arguments and fights safely; listening to both sides; when to involve an adult | Role-play a conflict scenario |
| 7 | Working as a team | Running meetings, planning events, sharing work, supporting each other | Plan and run a short council meeting |
| 8 | Accountability and reflection | Tracking duties, giving and receiving feedback, learning from mistakes | Weekly duty log and reflection |

Safety note for modules 5 and 6: student leaders should never physically intervene in a fight. The curriculum teaches them to call a teacher and keep other students away.

## 6. Implementation plan

1. **Baseline:** collect the "before" data (see section 7) before any training starts.
2. **Training:** deliver the modules to the council.
3. **Guided practice:** members perform their responsibilities in school while I coach them and check that duties are actually done.
4. **Review:** collect the "after" data, compare, and decide what to improve.

## 7. Measuring impact

The main question: **did the curriculum help members become confident leaders, and how did they feel as council members before and after?**

**Before and after, from the members**
- A short confidence survey, using the same questions before and after (for example, a 1 to 5 scale on "I know what my role is", "I know how to do my duties", "I feel confident speaking to other students", "I feel respected as a council member").
- Short written or spoken reflections: "How I feel as a council member" before and after.

**Observation**
- A simple checklist of whether members perform their responsibilities, completed during guided practice.

**School-level signals (supporting evidence, not proof on their own)**
- Number of fights or discipline incidents recorded per week.
- Teacher and peer feedback on whether students listen to the council.

Keep survey responses confidential where possible, and be honest in the final write-up about what the data does and does not show. Other things in the school may change at the same time.

## 8. What success looks like

- Every member can explain their role and their duties in their own words.
- Members report higher confidence after training than before.
- Duties are performed consistently, and the council has a visible routine.
- Fewer incidents, and students listen to the council more *(to be measured against the baseline)*.
